LMN — References & Methodology

Standards, assumptions, methodologies, and references behind the LMN neighbourhood-energy tool.

The information in this tool is produced by building-energy simulation under a defined set of assumptions and simplified calculations. Layers 3 (Mobility / EV-V2G) and 4 (Green Infrastructure / Landscape PV) are preliminary and still under active development — their values are based on simplified assumptions and are subject to refinement. Energy and PV results (Layers 1–2) are validated against published literature (see Validation) but remain modelling estimates.

A — What is LMN

LMN is an interactive neighbourhood-energy explorer. The user composes a neighbourhood by typology (Layer 1), then overlays energy demand + generation systems (Layer 2), electric mobility (Layer 3), and green infrastructure (Layer 4). Behind each neighbourhood archetype sits a full EnergyPlus simulation campaign across a ladder of energy-efficiency scenarios, so the user can compare code baselines against progressively deeper retrofits.

B — How the Four Layers Work

  • Layer 1 — Neighbourhood selection: choose land use, context, density, layout, and envelope code. Returns matching neighbourhood archetypes.
  • Layer 2 — Energy: energy demand systems (Heat Pump COP 4, Heat Pump for DHW, appliances) and on-site generation (PV). Shows EUI breakdown + PV generation.
  • Layer 3 — Mobility (preliminary): EV demand and V2G balance.
  • Layer 4 — Green infrastructure (preliminary): landscape PV (LPV) and green systems.

C — PV Methodology & the kWh/m² Definition

Two PV tracks

  • DEFAULT: native PV only — whatever the source prototype ships with. ≈ 0 kWh/m² for Canadian baselines; a few high-rise/ASHRAE prototypes carry a small native array (e.g. 1.9–3.3 kWh/m²).
  • EEM1–EEM4: a “Tier-3” injector adds rooftop PV sized from the actual roof geometry.

Sizing rules

  • Flat roofs → a single world-south rack at 45° tilt; active area = Σ(roof area) × GCR / cos(45°). The rack is corrected for the building’s North Axis so it always faces true south.
  • Pitched roofs (houses, some small commercial) → panels on the south-facing dominant pitched face only (dominant-face fallback if no south slope); array tilt = roof pitch.

Assumptions (identical for native + Tier-3)

Module efficiency 20% of aperture (pitched) / 200 W/m² roof power density (flat); ground-coverage ratio (GCR) 0.40; DC→AC system losses 14%; inverter efficiency 96%; azimuth = world south.

Units & normalization

PV intensity is reported as kWh per m² of gross (conditioned) floor area — the same denominator as EUI. It is not normalized by roof area, and not by footprint.

Why it varies from one neighbourhood to another: the numerator (roof PV generation) is limited by available roof area, while the denominator is the building’s total floor area. Low-rise forms (detached, row houses) have a large roof-area-per-floor-area ratio → high intensity; tall forms (mid-/high-rise) spread the same rooftop over many storeys → low intensity. This is physically expected, not an inconsistency. Representative NECB-2017 values: Detached (RC-D) ≈ 100.7, MidRise ≈ 66–75, HighRise (RC-HR1) ≈ 45.8, (RC-HR2) ≈ 26.0 kWh/m².

Total vs intensity are linked by: Total PV (kWh/yr) = PV intensity (kWh/m²) × gross floor area (m²). The PV page surfaces gross floor area and the absolute total alongside the intensity so the two are explicitly consistent.

E — Climate Zone & Weather

  • Modelled climate zone: ASHRAE CZ6A (cold, humid continental) — represented by Montréal. A colder CZ7A (Calgary) variant is also simulated.
  • Weather files: CWEC2020v2 Montréal Trudeau Intl AP (CZ6) and CWEC2020v2 Calgary Olympic Park Upper (CZ7A).
  • The DOE/PNNL prototype library has no Canadian city; Buffalo, NY (CZ6A) is used as the geometric proxy for US prototypes (Buffalo HDD₁₈ ≈ 3,500 vs Montréal ≈ 3,900; closest humid-climate match, preserves latent-cooling realism). US constructions/schedules are then replaced by NECB/NBC/BTAP equivalents for the Canadian baselines.

F — Building Archetypes & Sources

  • Commercial: 16 DOE/PNNL ASHRAE 90.1-2022 prototypes (offices, retail, schools, hotels, hospital, outpatient, restaurants, warehouse, apartment mid/high-rise) + 2 in-house high-rise variants (15, 20 storeys).
  • Residential: 3 IECC 2024 single-family variants (detached + garage, no-garage, row-house).
  • Supplemental (OpenStudio, 90.1-2019): College, Laboratory, TallBuilding, SuperTallBuilding, 2 small datacenters.
  • Datacenters: ASHRAE 90.1-2019 large-format prototypes (passed through, NECB-exempt).
  • Three large prototypes (Primary School, Secondary School, Warehouse) are used at 50% floor area to fit neighbourhood plots (see Resizing).

G — Energy Modelling Basis

  • Engine: EnergyPlus 22.1 (mid/high-rise + commercial) and 23.1 (single-family/attached).
  • EUI: site gross energy use intensity in kWh/m²·yr, normalized to conditioned floor area. PV generation is reported separately and never netted into EUI.
  • Neighbourhood airflow: AirflowNetwork:SimulationControl = MultizoneWithoutDistribution.
  • Envelope-code options offered per neighbourhood: Standard (NECB), Standard (ASHRAE), High Performance based on NECB, and High Performance based on ASHRAE. Note that the high-performance options represent high-performance envelope improvements (EEM1) implemented strictly on the building envelope for buildings built to either NECB or ASHRAE standards.

H — Layer 3: Mobility (EV / V2G) PRELIMINARY

Assumptions (from the tool’s Templates/NUS_EV.csv; currently the 9–10 RC neighbourhoods):

  • EV penetration: 1.5 EVs per household; daily energy demand 15 kWh/EV (≈ 200 km range); charging efficiency 90%.
  • V2G: participation 50%; battery efficiency 90%; discharge capacity 10 kW/day.
  • Reported metric: Net Energy Balance in kWh/m²·day, plus a grid-status indicator.
  • Status: preliminary, simplified — under development.

J — Resizing Methodology

Three oversized prototypes (Primary School, Secondary School, Warehouse) run at 50% floor area to fit the largest neighbourhood plots; geometry proportions and HVAC sizing ratios are preserved. High-rise storey variants (15/20) are derived by ZoneGroup multiplier with corrected zone Z-origins so solar and wind exposure stay physically correct.

K — Validation Summary

  • Whole-building EUI ladder validated against deep-energy-retrofit literature (NRCan PEER/BTAP, PNNL, NEEA/WSU): EEM3 ≈ −33% to −42%, EEM4 ≈ −52% to −62% vs baseline — within literature bands for 19 of 22 measures (2 conservative/under-credit, 1 no-direct-evidence).
  • Ideal-load (IAL) thermal demand validated against NRCan CEUD/NEUD and PNNL prototype baselines (heating, DHW, IAL/DEFAULT ratios all within expected ranges).

L — Limitations

Envelope & HVAC Modeling Constraints

  • AirflowNetwork Numerical Singularities: To prevent numerical singularities in the AirflowNetwork solver during high-wind winter events at the neighbourhood scale, the detailed duct distribution and leakage solver (`MultizoneWithDistribution`) is disabled. Neighbourhood simulations use the simpler `MultizoneWithoutDistribution` mode, dropping the duct leakage component (estimated to introduce a ≤ 5% EUI difference, but reducing simulation times tenfold).
  • Daylighting and Venetian Blinds Skip Rules: Daylighting controls are automatically skipped in zones with continuous-use schedules (e.g., life-safety/process) or specialty functions (labs, kitchens, surgical rooms) where dimming is impractical. Automated interior venetian blinds are skipped on Laboratory, Tall, and SuperTall buildings due to EnergyPlus v22.1 engine crash constraints.
  • Stratified Tank Reporting Anomaly (Phase P): In EnergyPlus v22.1, central stratified HPWH backup-resistance energy (≈ 7 kWh/m²·yr) is mis-bucketed under Space Heating instead of Water Heating. While the total combined energy is conserved, the individual end-use columns contain this reporting discrepancy.
  • Process-Load Dominated Typologies: Industrial and IT-heavy archetypes (e.g., Data Centers, Laboratories) exhibit low EUI sensitivity to typical envelope and HVAC EEM retrofits because their energy budgets are heavily dominated by IT plug loads and ventilation process exhaust, which are outside the scope of building-level EEMs.

Solar Photovoltaic (PV) Modeling Constraints

  • Facade PV Validation: Facade PV (building-integrated photovoltaics on vertical walls) is only applied to high-rise typologies (ApartmentHighRise, ST15, and ST20) where vertical area is significant. Additionally, facade PV yields have only been simulated and validated for Montreal (CZ6A), with Calgary (CZ7A) validation pending.
  • Pitched Commercial PV Sizing: Sizing values for pitched commercial buildings (OfficeSmall, RestaurantFastFood, RestaurantSitDown) are pending re-simulation under the south-facing dominant-pitched-roof constraint; current reporting reflects pre-fix values across all slopes.
  • Racking Tilt vs. Market Standards: While flat-roof PV racks are modeled at an energy-optimal tilt of 45° (or 20° for EEM4) for high-latitude winter performance, this tilt angle exceeds typical flat-roof commercial solar racking standards of 10–15°, which are constrained in practice by wind-uplift and structural ballast load limits.
  • Snow-Cover Losses: Current PV generation calculations do not account for snow accumulation, snow-shedding, or winter albedo gains, which may overestimate actual winter yields in cold Canadian climates.
  • No Electrical Storage or Grid Dynamics: The PV simulation layer assumes a simple baseload configuration where all generated DC electricity is inverted and fed directly into the building's electrical bus. No battery storage, peak-shaving control, or grid feed-in dynamics are modeled.
