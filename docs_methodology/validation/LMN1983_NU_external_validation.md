## 0. The finding that has to come first: two floor-area bases are in use

EnergyPlus reports site EUI on two denominators, `Energy Per Total Building Area` and `Energy Per Conditioned Building Area`. For the IECC single-family prototype they differ by **exactly a factor of 2** [9], because the attic and the unheated basement together are half the total area and neither is conditioned. RC-D's current-code cell reads `Total Building Area 21200.5 m2` against `Net Conditioned Building Area 10600.2 m2`.

Three parts of this project pick different denominators, and until now nothing reconciled them:

| Source | Denominator | RC-D / DetachedHouse current code |
|---|---|--:|
| Neighbourhood master CSVs (`onesquare.py` prefers `conditioned_floor_area`) [6] | net conditioned | 129.8 kWh/m2 |
| LMN-1983 building chapter (`LMN1983_validation_report.md`) [5] | total building area | 63.9 kWh/m2 |
| This run, same cell, total-area denominator | total building area | 64.9 kWh/m2 |

The building chapter's 63.9 and this run's 64.9 agree to within 1.6 per cent, which is the neighbourhood shading and adjacency effect and nothing more. **The two chapters were never in disagreement about the physics; they were reporting the same result on two different denominators.**

This matters because **every external benchmark is stated on gross floor area**: R13 [4] records `Gross floor area (175 m2 avg)` for the detached house and `Gross floor area (GFA)` for the MURBs, and RN02 [2] draws its composites from SCIEU and SHEU, which are GFA surveys. Checking the published conditioned-basis neighbourhood EUI against them would overstate the house-bearing neighbourhoods by about 2x and leave the apartment ones roughly unchanged, since a MURB has almost no unconditioned area. **Checks 2 and 3 below are therefore run on the total-area basis.** Check 1 stays on the conditioned basis because both of its sides come from the same pipeline and the same convention.

The conditioned-share column is the whole story: 0.50 for the pure-house neighbourhoods, close to 1.00 for the apartment ones. **The chapter's published delta is not affected** because it is a ratio between two arms measured on the same denominator, but every absolute EUI quoted against an external source is.

Every table below therefore carries **one** basis, named in its own heading. Checks 1 and 4 compare our pipeline against our own pipeline and stay on the conditioned basis it publishes; checks 2 and 3 compare against outside measured stock and are on gross floor area, which is the basis those sources state.

**OPEN ITEM, the HVAC-only measure is inert on the 1983 house neighbourhoods.** On the four purely house-built neighbourhoods here (RC-D, RC-ML, RC-R, RC-T) the isolated ASHP measure returns -0.3% on the 1983 arm against -20.6% on the current-code arm of the same neighbourhoods. RC-MR1, which carries apartment blocks, does not show it (-21.2% on the 1983 arm). The measure is therefore reaching the heating equipment of the current-code house and not reaching it on the 1983 house. That is a difference between the two appliers, not a physical result, and it is not explained by anything in this check. It also depresses the 1983 EEM3 column, which lands at -30.0% against -35.8% on the current-code arm, the opposite of the expected ordering. **Nothing in the submitted results document should quote the 1983 HVAC-only or EEM2 figures for these four neighbourhoods until this is traced.** The cumulative EEM1 and the baseline are unaffected.

The same signature is visible in table 4.2 for the same four neighbourhoods (HVAC 1983 near zero while every apartment-bearing neighbourhood returns -30.7% or better), so it is an archetype-linked effect rather than a neighbourhood one.

**Reading of check 4.** On the isolated view the envelope measure moves by a median of -11.5 percentage points between the two arms while the DHW measure moves by 4.8 in the opposite direction. That is the expected shape. The envelope is what 1983 got wrong, so upgrading it returns a larger share of a larger baseline. The water heater is unchanged between the two vintages, so it saves a similar number of kWh either way, and expressing that as a percentage of the much larger 1983 baseline makes it look smaller.

On the four neighbourhoods the fall-through never touched, our current-code baseline reproduces the published 2017 baseline to a median ratio of 1.000, which is the reproducibility floor of the pipeline across a three-month gap in code. On the five affected ones the ratio is 0.938, and that difference is the defect, not physics.

## 5. Reading

**1. The pipeline closes.** Check 1 is 9 of 9 PASS with a median ratio of 0.980 and a spread of 0.955 to 1.016. A neighbourhood of N copies of an archetype reproduces that archetype's own standalone NECB 2017 EUI to within a few per cent, so the merge, the placement, the shading and the plant consolidation are not injecting energy. The consistent small negative bias on the apartment neighbourhoods (RC-HR1 0.966, RC-HR2 0.969, RC-MR2 0.980) is mutual shading, which is present in a neighbourhood and absent in a standalone run and can only reduce cooling and solar gain. That is the expected sign.

**2. Against measured Quebec stock the 1983 arm sits at about 0.72, not at 1.0, and that is the same result the building chapter already published.** Checks 2 and 3 agree with each other (0.720 and 0.728 median) despite using different references, which is what makes the number credible rather than a benchmark artefact. The building chapter's fleet median was r83 = 0.89 with the two houses at 0.64 and 0.70; the RC family is house dominated by floor area, so a family median near 0.72 is exactly what those per-archetype figures predict. **This is not a new problem discovered at neighbourhood scale. It is the known prototype-library gap, reproduced.**

**3. The split inside check 3 is informative and points at one archetype.** The apartment neighbourhoods land close to their measured medians while the house-bearing ones fall short. RN01 predicted precisely this: Montreal's pre-war plex fabric is absent from the prototype library and the IECC AttachedHouse proxy underestimates real pre-1983 plex heating intensity: 210 kWh/m2.yr measured against 125 simulated (RN01 rows B10 and B11 [3], source Hydro-Québec residential load survey 2018; Concordia UBEM dataset (Famitch 2021)). RN01 states that gap as approximately 35 per cent; the two rows themselves give 40.5 per cent of the measured value, and the smaller figure is used wherever it is quoted, as the conservative one. The shortfall is concentrated where RN01 said it would be.

**4. What this does and does not license.** The percentage deltas between the two arms stay the defensible number, because both arms are measured on the same denominator and against the same prototype library, so the library gap cancels. The absolute EUIs are prototype-relative and read low against measured stock. That is the same wording the building chapter's T10 report [5] already carries, and this check extends it from 31 buildings to 9 neighbourhoods rather than revising it.

**5. One decision is owed to the user, and it is not a detail.** The chapter's submitted results document quotes the conditioned-area EUI (RC-D 1983 = 177.7) while every external source it might be read against is on gross floor area (RC-D 1983 = 88.9). Both are correct; only one is comparable. The options are to keep the conditioned basis and footnote it, to switch the reported basis to GFA, or to publish both columns. Nothing has been changed in the submitted document pending that call.
